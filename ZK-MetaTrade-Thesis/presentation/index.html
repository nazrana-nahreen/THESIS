01. Problem Background

The Metaverse & The Rise of AI-Generated Content (AIGC)

Exponential Demand

Virtual worlds require millions of 3D meshes, neural avatars, textures, and behavior models. Traditional manual creation (PGC/UGC) cannot scale; AIGC is the indispensable foundation.

Access vs. Scarcity (NFTs)

Unlike 1-of-1 collectibles, AIGCs are access-based digital files. Thousands of users concurrently license the same asset or AI weight checkpoint.

Centralization Trap

Existing Digital Asset Management (DAM) platforms rely on trusted 3rd-party brokers, introducing Single Points of Failure (SPoF), high commissions (30%), and insider theft risks.

Research Goal: Design a completely decentralized, mathematically trust-free exchange protocol where buyer and seller cannot cheat each other, even without an arbitrator.
02. State-of-the-Art Baseline

MetaTrade (IEEE Access 2024): The Commit-and-Compare Design

MetaTrade 3-Phase Exchange Protocol:

  • 1. Deposit: Customer deposits license fee into DigitalAssetContract.sol escrow via registerRequest().
  • 2. Encrypt & Commit: Seller encrypts data with $K^{sym}$, computes ciphertext hash $H = \text{SHA256}(D_{enc})$, encrypts key with buyer's public key $K_{signed}$, and posts to IPFS.
  • 3. Compare: Buyer downloads $D_{enc}$, computes $H_c$, and invokes compareHashes(H_c) on-chain.
  • 4. Settle: If $H_c == H$, contract releases funds to seller and grants key to buyer.

MetaTrade's Academic Claim:

"MetaTrade eliminates the role of the trusted third party, without requiring trust assumptions among participants... security analysis reveals that the framework is resilient against trust-related attacks."
Our Finding: This claim is mathematically broken. A malicious seller can extract 100% of the buyer's funds with zero penalty.
Reference: Truong et al., IEEE Access, vol. 12, pp. 41815–41828, Mar. 2024.
03. Vulnerability Analysis

Vulnerability 1: The "Poison Key" Fair-Exchange Exploit

Mathematical Formulation of the Attack

The smart contract checks only whether $H_c == H$ (ciphertext hash match). It cannot verify whether the submitted key actually decrypts the ciphertext!

1. Malicious seller generates true key $K^{real} \to D_{enc}$.
2. Ciphertext commitment: $H = \text{SHA256}(D_{enc})$.
3. Seller crafts Poison Key: $K^{poison} \neq K^{real}$.
4. Encrypts $K^{poison} \to K_{signed}^{bogus} = \text{Enc}_{pk_B}(K^{poison})$.
5. Buyer downloads $D_{enc}$, confirms $H_c \equiv H$!
6. Contract releases 1.0 ETH to Malicious Seller!
7. Decryption: $\text{AES-GCM}(D_{enc}, K^{poison}) \to \mathbf{FAIL}$.

04. Vulnerability Analysis

Vulnerabilities 2 & 3: Synchronous Bottleneck & Monolithic Royalties

#2

Synchronous Seller Online Bottleneck

MetaTrade requires the seller to actively listen to blockchain events, fetch the buyer's public key, encrypt the symmetric key, and invoke grantAccess() on-chain for every individual purchase.

• If seller sleeps/disconnects: Buyer funds locked indefinitely.
• High latency: Trading cannot scale to metaverse demand.
• Violates non-interactive electronic commerce requirements.
#3

Monolithic Single-Parent Royalty Constraint

Modern AIGC models are inherently compositional (Base SDXL + Style LoRA + ControlNet Depth Rig + Prompts). MetaTrade declares:

DigitalAssetContract public parentAsset; // Exactly ONE parent!

Only 1 parent address can receive commissions. Multi-tier contributors, pipeline orchestrators, and adapter trainers are left completely uncompensated.

05. Consortium Blockchain Audit

Vulnerabilities in MetaTrade's Hyperledger Fabric Chaincode

Read-Your-Own-Writes (RYOW) Ghost Token Inflation

In Fabric, getState() reads uncommitted snapshot DB values. Calling _transfer twice from escrow in one transaction causes the second transfer to read stale state ($100$ instead of $90$), writing $100 - 90 = 10$ into escrow!

Empirical Result: Total supply inflated from 1000 to 1010 tokens out of thin air!

Re-Registration Escrow Locking Bug

registerRequest transferred funds to composite escrow before checking if reqId existed. Duplicate or retried invocations accumulated multiple deposits, but compareHashes only disbursed one req.price!

Empirical Result: 100 tokens permanently locked in digital_asset_req_car1 with zero refund path!
Thesis Finding: Proved live on our 30-orderer Raft consortium testbed. Both vulnerabilities resolved via our _transferMulti aggregation engine.

08. Core Contribution 2

Multi-Parent DAG Micro-Royalty Distribution Engine

Mathematical Formulation

Let asset $\mathcal{A}$ depend on $k$ ancestor models $\mathcal{P} = \{P_1, \dots, P_k\}$ with weights $\mathbf{w} = [w_1, \dots, w_k]$ in basis points ($\sum w_i \le 5000$).

\text{Royalty}(P_i) = \frac{P \cdot w_i}{10000}, \quad \forall i \in \{1, \dots, k\}
\text{Payout}(\mathcal{S}) = P - \sum_{i=1}^k \text{Royalty}(P_i)

Executed via iterative low-level transfers to each parent contract, updating balances deterministically in $O(k)$ time.

09. Core Contribution 3

Fabric Concurrency Remediation: Atomic In-Memory Multi-Transfer

The _transferMulti Solution

To overcome Fabric's Read-Your-Own-Writes limitation, we engineered _transferMulti(ctx, from, transfers) inside tokenERC20.js:

1. Checks fromBalance >= total (Insufficient funds check)

Formal Proofs & Strict Nash Equilibrium

Strong Fair Exchange Theorem

Theorem: ZK-MetaTrade guarantees Strong Fair Exchange.

  • Atomic Settlement: Escrow disburses funds if and only if buyer receives a verified, decryptable symmetric key.
  • No Arbitrator: Neutralizes disputes deterministically on-chain without third-party human or oracle bias.

Honest Key + Proof (P - C_s, V_b - P) ★ (0, -G_b)
